Budgeting

Yearly budgets for your club's recurring costs: the cycle, the rules, and how to enter it.

  • Overview
  • Funding Requests
  • After Your FR
  • Budgeting
  • Club Classifications
  • Treasurer Toolkit
  • Test Your Knowledge

Budgets cover your club's recurring yearly costs. They are submitted during C-term for the next fiscal year (July through June). Only Class I, II, and III organizations can submit one. Not sure of your class? See Club Classifications.

Before you can submit: a representative from your club's executive board has to attend a budgeting workshop held during C-term. You can sign up for one under Events on myWPI.

The budgeting cycle

The budgeting cycle
StepWhat happens
1. WorkshopAttend a budgeting workshop during C-term. Workshops cover the rules, what can go in a budget, and how to enter it.
2. SubmitEnter your budget on your club's myWPI page under budgeting. The portal opens in January and stays open for about a month.
3. ReviewThe Appropriations Committee reviews every budget and returns them to clubs in early March.
4. Appeal (optional)If line items were cut or changed, you can appeal (see below).
5. SpendYour budget is available at the start of the fiscal year, July 1.
6. Prove itSubmit proof of spending for every line item (see below).

Last cycle (FY27) for reference: the portal opened January 14 and closed February 13. Budgets were returned March 4, appeals were due March 18, and appeal decisions came back March 23.

The rules

What can go in a budget

Rule 1

Annual costs only

If you will only pay an expense this year, or will not pay it again for two years, it belongs in a funding request. For example, dodgeballs are reusable, so they are not an annual cost.

Rule 2

A track record

Each line item has to be in one of your last two fiscal years' budgets, or have been approved as a funding request in each of the last two fiscal years.

Rule 3

Follows policy

Everything follows the Fiscal Policy and University policy. See the Fiscal Policy summary and the University policies on the Treasurer Toolkit.

Rule 4

Clear cost breakdowns

Each line item should have enough detail to remove any guesswork. Some costs are hard to predict, but try not to overestimate. Descriptions matter a lot.

Budgeting rules on top of the Fiscal Policy

  • No more than a 5% increase per line item
  • No single vendor (a big artist or external speaker, for example) costing over $10,000
  • No trips over $125 per person per day, counting everything SGA funds (transportation, lodging, registration, and so on)
  • No single event costing over $10,000 per day
  • No funding for food that is not from Chartwells
  • Up to $250 per fiscal year for membership recruitment
  • Competitive Class II clubs can get up to 60% of transportation costs, or $60 per estimated WPI undergraduate attending (whichever is higher), for in-season competitions and matches
How-to

Building your budget

Your budget is made up of one or more Budget Requests. Each request has a type, and each one contains one or more line items that detail the costs, and each line item has its own type too.

Budget Request types

  • Event, Off Campus (an off-campus event you are hosting)
  • Event, On Campus (an on-campus event you are hosting)
  • Competitions, Off Campus (matches, tournaments, and so on)
  • Competitions, On Campus
  • Conferences and Trips, Off Campus (national and regional conferences, trips)
  • Equipment and Supplies (new equipment, maintenance, software, printing)
  • Apparel (one annual apparel item)
  • Operational Expense (fees, group registration, licensing, coaches fees)
  • Other

Line item types

  • Coaches Fees, Competition Fees, Lesson Fees, Registration Fees, Annual Dues
  • Software, Space Rental, Trainings, Facilities, Police, EMS
  • External Artist or Speaker, A/V (non LNL), LNL, Programming
  • Food, Apparel, Lodging, Transportation, Marketing and Advertising
  • New Equipment, Equipment Maintenance, Expendable Supplies (used once), Reusable Supplies (used more than once)
  • Other (Expense)

Before you use "Other" for anything, email the Appropriations Chair at sgaappropchair@wpi.edu with the item. If several clubs need the same thing, we add it as its own option.

  1. Go to your club's myWPI page and click Manage.
  2. Open your Accounting Book.
  3. Select the current SGA Budget and create a new Budget Request.
  4. Fill in the request: the type, a descriptive title, a 2 to 3 sentence description, and a priority (how essential it is to your club).
  5. Add each line item with its type, the amount requested from SGA, the amount your group is paying, and notes on what it is being spent on (1 to 2 sentences at least).
  6. Say whether the item came from a previous budget or funding request, and explain why it is allowed in your budget this year.
  7. Attach one PDF of all invoices related to the request, and answer the start date, end date, and expected number of undergraduate participants.
  8. Click Save when you have added all the line items for that request.
Appeals

Budget appeals

You can appeal any line item the Appropriations Committee cut or changed. Submit the Budget Appeals survey on myWPI by the appeal deadline. You may be asked to present your appeal to the Budget Appeals Board, which is made up of Financial Board, organizational representatives, and additional representatives from Class I, II, and III organizations.

Appeal decisions are final. You can still ask for money for those items through a Funding Request.

After the budget

After you have a budget

You have to submit what you spent and proof of spending for every line item. If a payment request is not submitted for a line item, that line item will be cut from your budget next year.

  • Pick the transaction type: purchase order, purchase requisition, invoice, P-Card, or personal reimbursement.
  • Describe what the line item was used for, and put "Expense" for revenue or expense.
  • Enter how much SGA money you spent on the line item, and how much came from group funds.
  • Upload proof of spending. The amount on your receipts has to match what you entered.
  • Enter the date the funds were spent (the last day, if it spread across several days) and the number of undergraduates who benefited.

A reallocation moves money from one or more line items in your budget to another line item or to a new, non-budget item. Class I, II, and III organizations can request one.

  • An executive officer fills out the Budget Reallocation Request Form at least 2 weeks before the funds are needed, and says which line item in the current fiscal year's budget the money is coming from.
  • The Treasurer decides whether to hear the request.
  • If the money moves between existing line items, the Accountant, Appropriations Chair, and Fiscal Responsibility and Assistance Chair hear it. If it goes to a new line item, Financial Board, Senate, or the Executive Board hears it, at the Treasurer's discretion.
  • You do not need to fill out a report form after a reallocation is approved.
